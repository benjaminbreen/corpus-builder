Arithmometer — a device used for the mechanical execution of large computations, or a calculating machine.

The history of the discovery of the arithmometer begins in the deepest antiquity; in almost every period of human development we see attempts to find a way to облегчить вычисления by means of automatic contrivances. In the ancient period of history, when the use of ancient numeral signs presented many inconveniences, the so‑called abacus [Абакос] was invented (see that entry); or a counting board, which was used not only by children but also by mathematicians and astronomers. Among the Chinese, in turn, there was in common use a counting device resembling in form the Russian abacus of our own time, which greatly облегчал computations in the mind. The later discovery of logarithms and their application to complex arithmetic computations is a major step toward a method by which we could perform and verify our calculations. At the same time we see the efforts of many inventors directed toward building such a calculating machine that would require of a person no other knowledge than the reading of numeral signs. In the period from the beginning of the 17th century to the present time one can count innumerable calculating apparatuses, partly for general and partly for special computations.

All such calculating apparatuses, or arithmometers [арифмометры] as they are commonly called, can be reduced to two main types: to the first type belong devices that merely shorten and облегчить the mental exertions of a person, whereas devices of the second type perform the most complex computations without any participation of human reason, by means of known manipulations, and can rather be called automatic counters.

Among arithmometers of the first type we may point to the devices of Edmond Gunter (invented in 1624) and Gaspar Schott (1668). Both made use of the discovery of logarithmic tables, which the former arranged on a circle and the latter on movable cylinders, so that with a very simple contrivance one immediately obtains the results of multiplication and division of large numbers. To this type must also be assigned Napier’s rods [rabdology], Lalande’s arithmoplanimeter (1839), and many others which, differing in their construction, had at their base the same idea — by means of a simple contrivance to облегчить and shorten the performance of complex operations with large numbers.

The invention of arithmometers of the second type is entirely the achievement of our century. The best representative of this type must undoubtedly be признан the arithmometer of the Alsatian Thomas, invented in 1820, as satisfying all just требования of an automatic counter and as having acquired universal use in practical mathematics, despite the complexity of its construction. In the drawing appended here we provide a schematic representation of this ingenious device. By moving the pointers C we set the given number that is to be subjected to a given operation; the handle, which sets in motion a whole system of toothed wheels, transfers the given number into the numerators E; the second number is then again set on the pointers C, and by means of the same handle, observing certain rules, the result of the operations to be performed on the given numbers is obtained in the numerators E. The Thomas arithmometer, in addition to all four basic operations of arithmetic, performs exponentiation, logarithmation, and other computations, and all operations are absolutely correct and mathematically precise. But the principal and invaluable достоинство of Thomas’s device must be recognized in the circumstance that anyone can easily use it without special mathematical knowledge; the device is sufficiently simple and does not cause fatigue in prolonged use. Without entering into details of the construction of Thomas’s arithmometer and the methods of working with it, we refer the interested reader to the articles: “Instruction pour se servir de l’Arithmomètre, inventée par Thomas” (Paris, 1851) and “La grande Encyclopédie,” vol. Among arithmometers of Russian origin we may point to the devices of our famous academician P. L. Chebyshev, the Jewish scholar H. Z. Slonimsky, and the newest design, the arithmometer of V. T. Odhner [Однер], invented in 1890. We place on the appended plate a drawing of Odhner’s arithmometer at 1/4 natural size. Let us остановимся in detail on the construction of this device and the method of its use. The handle B is connected to a cylinder to which are attached spokes that emerge from the slots A of the casing. The spokes are set in different positions relative to each other along the slots. The initial position of the cylinder is indicated by the vertical position of the handle; in this position the handle is held by a spring, therefore it must be released for rotation. The initial position of the cylinder is also the initial position of the spokes, indicating zero. By shifting the spokes, one can set all digits on the cover from 0 to 9; for облегчение of setting digits the slots are numbered from right to left. The box contains two systems of windows; in the large windows appear the digits set before the rotation of the handle by the spokes on the cover, as well as the result of addition or subtraction. The digits in the small windows show the difference in the number of turns of the handle in both directions (arrow + and arrow −), in other words, a control over the number of turns of the handle. The entire box, as needed, is shifted by pressing button D, whereupon a latch falls into the slots, holding the box. The final position is indicated by dots located above the windows, namely: if one of the dots is under the arrow on the left side of the cover, the latch falls into the slots and holds the box. The box is shifted only when the handle is in the vertical position, the motion of which is possible only when the box is in the above‑mentioned position. The clearing of the digits in the box’s large windows is done by rotating the right “swallow” C and in the small windows by rotating the left “swallow” C. The “swallows” must constantly be in their initial position, indicated by recesses. The manipulation of Odhner’s arithmometer reduces to the following four points: setting digits on the cover, turning the handle, shifting the box, and turning the “swallows.” On the basis of these four operations one solves problems for all four rules of arithmetic.

We shall give several examples illustrating the use of Odhner’s arithmometer. Suppose one needs to find the sum: 75384 + 6278 + 6278 + 9507. The handle must first be in the initial position and the digits in the windows must show zero. Having set 75384 on the spokes, the handle is turned in the direction of the + arrow one time; then, having set 6278, the handle is turned in the same direction two times; having then set 9507 again and turned the handle, the large windows will show the number 97447 — the required sum. The small windows show the number 4, indicating only the quantity of turns of the handle. To find the product 49563 × 24? Since the product consists of the 24‑fold sum of the number 49563, one must set the number 49563 on the cover and make 24 turns of the handle in the direction of the + arrow. The shifting of the box allows one to reduce the number of turns by 4 + 2 = 6. Having made 4 turns, the box is shifted to the next dot under the arrow on the left side of the cover and the handle is turned two more times, whereupon the large windows of the box show the result 1,189,512 and the small windows show the multiplier 24. At the beginning of the operation, of course, all windows must show 0. It is easy to guess that for subtraction one uses the − arrow, and that division is shortened subtraction, reduced on the device to the action of the latter (on arithmometers of other kinds see the articles: Babbage, Integrators, and “Addition”).
